## at, in

which one is better, why?

1. Can I find second-hand books **at** the **flea market**.
2. Can I find second-hand books **in** the **flea market**.

In the first sentence, "**at**" is used to suggest that the second-hand books can be found within the **physical location** of the flea market.

In the second sentence, "**in**" is used to suggest that the second-hand books can be found inside the **broader concept** of the flea market.

Overall, both sentences are correct and can be used interchangeably, but the first sentence using "at" is slightly more specific about the location where the books can be found.

## for, on, in

which is better:

1. We'll be meeting at our place **for** the Mid-Autumn Festival.
2. We'll be meeting at our place **on** the Mid-Autumn Festival.
3. We'll be meeting at our place **in** the Mid-Autumn Festival.

Out of the three options you provided, "We'll be meeting at our place on the Mid-Autumn Festival" is the most appropriate and commonly used phrasing.

The preposition "on" is typically used when referring to a specific date or day of an event, such as "on Christmas Day" or "on Easter Sunday". Since the Mid-Autumn Festival is a specific date in the calendar, "on" is the most fitting preposition to use in this context.

Using "in" can imply a more general time period, such as "in the summer" or "in the morning", so it is less appropriate for referring to a specific date.

Using "for" can imply a purpose or reason, such as "for the purpose of celebrating the Mid-Autumn Festival", which may not be the intended meaning.

Therefore, "We'll be meeting at our place on the Mid-Autumn Festival" is the clearest and most accurate way to convey the intended meaning.
