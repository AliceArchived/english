# 2. Shopping

## 1. General

1. 这是艾玛在商店买的所有东西吗？
   1. **Is this everything** Emma bought **at** the shop?
2. 他在拿你的钱包时，你在商店里吗？
   1. Were you **in** the store while he was getting your wallet? (~~at~~)
      1. the preposition "at" could refer to being in the general **vicinity** or proximity of the store, but not necessarily inside it.
      2. "in" which implies being inside a building
3. She's going to **get** a pair of **earrings** today.
4. Were you **here** while someone was stealing your wallet?
5. He is stealing money from **the** store now.
6. 我们在购物时，有人偷了她的手提包。
   1. While we were shopping, someone **stole** her purse.
7. 我找到了我想要的所有东西。
   1. I found all the things I wanted.
   2. I found **everything** I wanted.
   3. both ok
8. She likes the earrings that you **bought her**.
9. 她在找她的手提包时弄丢了一串钥匙。
   1. She lost **a set of keys** while she was looking for her purse.
10. Did you buy any furniture at the second-hand shop?
11. 首饰盒是在那里吗？
    1. **Are** the **jewelry boxes** over there?
12. 我看到各种太阳镜！
    1. I see all kinds of sunglasses!
13. The **sunglasses** are not very expensive.
14. These sunglasses are half-price, aren't they?
15. Most of these sunglasses are half-price.
16. This second-hand shop isn't closed, is it?
17. Do you prefer **silver jewelry** or **gold jewelry**?
    1. Do you prefer silver jewellery or gold jewellery?
18. Is everything half-price?
19. There's a man over there who sells caps.
20. There are bags at the second-hand shop, aren't there?

## 2. Grocery

1. 你能在杂货店买一袋葡萄吗？
   1. Could you get a bag of grapes at the ~~grocery~~?
      1. sounds more natural and is more commonly used in American English.
   2. Could you get a bag of **grapes** at the **grocer**?
   3. In American English, the word "**grocery**" is typically used to refer to **the store** that sells food and household items, while "**grocer**" refers to the **person** who owns or runs the store. Therefore, the first option is better suited to convey the intended meaning of asking someone to buy a bag of grapes at a store.
   4. However, in British English, the word "**grocer**" is still commonly used to refer to the store that sells food and household items. In this case, both options are correct, but the second option "Could you get a bag of grapes at the grocer?" would be more appropriate in British English.
2. 十二个水瓶
   1. twelve **water bottles**
3. 在我们的公寓对面有一个杂货店**。**
   1. There's a grocery store **across from** our apartment.
4. 我们需要列一个购物清单吗？
   1. Do we need to **create** a shopping list?
5. 你可以买一些奶酪还有一袋饼干吗？
   1. Would you like to buy some cheese and a bag of biscuits?
   2. Can you **get** some cheese and **also** a bag of **crackers**?
6. 我在回家路上**去了一趟**书店。
   1. I **stopped at** the bookstore on my way home.
7. 你在回家路上可以**去一趟**杂货店么？
   1. Can you **stop at** the grocery store on your way home?
