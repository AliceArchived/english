       - **Definition**: "Sore" generally describes a persistent, dull, or aching pain. It often refers to discomfort that comes from overuse, strain, or inflammation.

       - **Definition**: "Hurt" is a more general term that describes pain, injury, or damage. It can refer to both acute and chronic pain.
       - **Usage**: It can describe pain that is sudden, sharp, or severe, and it often implies a direct cause or injury.
       - Example:
         - If you accidentally slam your thumb in a door and feel a sharp pain, you might say, "My thumb hurts."

    4. ### Comparison

       - **Severity**: "Hurt" often implies more immediate or intense pain compared to "sore," which suggests a milder, more persistent discomfort.
       - **Duration**: "Sore" usually indicates discomfort that develops over time, often from overuse, whereas "hurt" can refer to immediate pain from an injury.
       - **Context**: "Sore" is often used in contexts where pain is associated with exercise, repetitive movement, or minor inflammation. "Hurt" is broader and can apply to any situation where there is pain, including sudden injuries.
