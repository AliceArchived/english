# The Debate Club - B3

Zari is walking toward the library when she notices Lily standing with the president of the debat club. Zari stops and stares.

- **Zari**: What is Lily doing? She hates debate club!

Lily is talking to the debate club president and pointing at something on her phone.

- **Zari**: Are they… friends now?! No, it can't be.

Zari walks closer, trying to listen.

- **Zari**: Lily doesn't even like clubs… Except our band, of course.

Lily laughs at something the debate club president says. Zari gasps.

- **Zari**: Oh no! Is Lily leaving our band to join the debate team?

Zari hides behind a bookshelf, still watching.

- **Zari**: Maybe she thinks debate club is more "important." Or maybe the president has **some kind of influence over** her.

Lily looks up and sees Zari **peeking** around the corner.

- **Lily**: Zari, why are you hiding over there?

Zari **freezes** and **steps out** slowly.

- **Zari**: I, uh… I wasn't hiding. I was just… looking for a book.
- **Lily**: Right.
- **Zari**: Were you talking to the debate club president?
- **Lily**: Yeah.
- **Zari**: Why?! You hate debate.
- **Lily**: I know. I just asked to borrow the debate club's **projector**.
- **Zari**: What?!
- **Lily**: I'm setting up a horror movie night for us.
- **Zari**: Oh! For us? Really?!
- **Lily**: Yeah. Relax, Zari. I'm not joining debate club.
- **Zari**: Thank goodness. Because I'd never survive that **soundtrack of endless talking**.
- **Lily**: Yeah, me neither.

## Vocabulary

- Maybe she thinks debate club is more "important." Or maybe the president has **some kind of influence over** her.
  - influence: effect
- Why?! You hate debate.
  - debate: argument

## Speaking & Writing

### 🌟 Speaking Band 7.0+

**Why was Lily talking to the debate president?**

Actually, Lily was only talking to the debate club president because she wanted to borrow their projector. She wasn’t planning to join the club at all. She explained that she needed the projector to set up a horror movie night for her and her friends. So, even though Zari thought Lily might be switching clubs, the truth was much simpler. Lily was just arranging something fun for the her and her friends, not showing any real interest in debate.

其实，Lily 和辩论社社长说话只是因为她想借投影仪。她完全没有打算加入辩论社。她解释说，她需要投影仪来为自己和朋友们举办一场恐怖电影之夜。所以，尽管 Zari 以为 Lily 可能会转到别的社团，事实却要简单得多。Lily 只是想为她和她的朋友们安排一件有趣的活动，并没有对辩论表现出真正的兴趣。

> Explanation:

This version is **fluent**, uses **a natural speaking style** with fillers like “actually” and “so,” and develops the answer with **reasoning and contrast**. Sentence length is varied, vocabulary is flexible but not overly formal, and ideas are clearly connected. These features are typical of Band 7+ speaking.

### 🌟 Writing Band 7.0+

**Why was Lily talking to the debate president?**

In the story, Lily was speaking to the debate club president not because she was interested in debate, but because she needed to borrow the club’s projector. Her intention was to use it for organizing a horror movie night for her friends. This detail highlights a misunderstanding between the characters: while Zari assumed Lily might be leaving the band for debate club, the reality was that Lily was only preparing something enjoyable for her friends. Therefore, her conversation with the debate club president was purely practical and had nothing to do with joining another club or changing her interests.

在故事中，Lily 和辩论社社长交谈并不是因为她对辩论感兴趣，而是因为她需要借用社团的投影仪。她的目的是用它来为朋友们组织一场恐怖电影之夜。这个细节凸显了人物之间的误会：Zari 以为 Lily 可能会离开乐队加入辩论社，而事实上，Lily 只是为她的朋友准备一些有趣的活动。因此，她与辩论社社长的对话完全是出于实际需要，与加入其他社团或改变兴趣无关。

> Explanation:

This version is **more formal and logically structured**, using academic connectors like “not because…but because,” “this detail highlights,” and “therefore.” The argument is developed clearly and with precision, which fits Band 7+ writing standards. Sentence structures are complex and vocabulary shows range.

### Speaking vs Writing Feature Comparison

| Feature                 | Speaking Version                                       | Writing Version                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sentence length         | Short to medium; varied (10–20 words)                  | Longer, more complex (20–30 words)                                |
| Tone                    | Conversational, relaxed, explanatory                   | Formal, structured, academic                                      |
| Common connectors       | “Actually,” “so,” “even though”                        | “Not because…but because,” “this detail highlights,” “therefore”  |
| Vocabulary choice       | Everyday words: “fun,” “just,” “truth”                 | Precise words: “intention,” “organizing,” “misunderstanding”      |
| Emotional expression    | Shows surprise/contrast (“the truth was much simpler”) | Neutral, analytical (“purely practical,” “nothing to do with…”)   |
| Rhetorical/detail style | Storytelling, contrast, simplification of Zari’s worry | Analytical, explanation of misunderstanding, formal clarification |
