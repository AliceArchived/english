# The Party Helper - B3

Zari walks into the gym for the **going-away party** of Hannah, a girl at their school. She **freezes** when she sees Lily.

- **Zari**: Lily? What are you doing here? You hate parties!
- **Lily**: I know.

Lily is holding a box of decorations.

- **Zari**: Are you… helping with the party?
- **Lily**: Yeah.

Zari looks horrified.

- **Zari**: Why?! You always say Hannah is mean and annoying.
- **Lily**: She is.
- **Zari**: Then why are you helping her?
- **Lily**: I have a reason.

Zari gasps.

- **Zari**: Oh no. Are you moving away with her?! I can hardly believe it!
- **Lily**: What? Zari, no.
- **Zari**: Then why? Just tell me! Do you like her now? Are you **trying to fit in** with her friends?
- **Lily**: Zari, calm down.

Lily **reaches into** her bag and **pulls out** a DVD.

- **Lily**: Hannah borrowed my favorite horror movie weeks ago. If I don't help, I won't get it back before she moves.
- **Zari**: That's it?
- **Lily**: Yeah.

Zari shakes her head, then laughs.

- **Zari**: You could also download it, you know.
- **Lily**: No, I want the original. And **you're welcome for helping at this awful party**.
- **Zari**: You're right. You hate this stuff. Thanks for putting up with it… for me.
- **Lily**: **Don't get used to it**.

## Vocabulary

- calm: relax

## Speaking

### 🌟 Speaking Band 7.0+

#### 2025-08-24

**Why did Lily help with the party?**

Lily **only** helped with the party **because** she wanted something back from Hannah. A few weeks earlier, Hannah had borrowed Lily’s favorite horror movie, and Lily **realized** that if she didn’t **help out**, she might never see it again once Hannah moved away. Honestly, she wasn’t interested in the party itself — in fact, she hates parties — but she was **motivated** to get her DVD back. It shows she was being **practical** rather than suddenly becoming friendly.

Lily 之所以帮忙办派对，只是因为她想要从 Hannah 那里拿回东西。几周前，Hannah 借走了她最喜欢的恐怖电影，Lily 意识到如果她不帮忙，等 Hannah 搬走就可能再也拿不回来了。其实，她对派对本身毫无兴趣——事实上她讨厌派对——但她为了拿回 DVD 才这么做。这说明她是出于实际考虑，而不是突然和 Hannah 交好。

> Explanation:

This answer reaches Band 7+ for Speaking because it uses natural spoken connectors (“Honestly,” “in fact”), shows expansion beyond the basic fact, adds emphasis with contrasts, and uses idiomatic expressions (“get her DVD back,” “was being practical”). Tone is conversational and fluent.

### 🌟 Writing Band 7.0+

#### 2025-08-24

**Why did Lily help with the party?**

Lily’s decision to help with the party had little to do with **friendship** or **enthusiasm** for the event. Instead, it was **entirely practical**. Several weeks earlier, Hannah had borrowed Lily’s favorite horror movie. **With** Hannah about to move away, Lily **understood** that helping with the preparations was the only way to ensure she could **retrieve** it in time. Although Lily dislikes parties and finds them unpleasant, she was willing to **set aside** her feelings for a clear purpose. Her involvement demonstrates a **pragmatic** rather than **emotional motivation**, highlighting her practical attitude toward the situation.

Lily 决定帮忙办派对与友谊或对活动的热情无关，而是出于完全实际的考虑。几周前，Hannah 借走了她最喜欢的恐怖电影。随着 Hannah 即将搬走，Lily 明白只有帮忙准备派对，才能及时拿回电影。尽管 Lily 讨厌派对并觉得它们令人不快，她还是为了明确的目的暂时放下了个人感受。她的参与表现出一种务实而非情感上的动机，突显了她对这件事情的实际态度。

> Explanation:

This answer fits Band 7+ for Writing because it develops a clear argument, uses formal vocabulary (“pragmatic,” “retrieve,” “enthusiasm”), employs complex structures (e.g., concessive clause “Although Lily dislikes parties…”), and shows logical coherence. The tone is more academic and structured than the Speaking version.

### Speaking vs Writing Feature Comparison

#### 2025-08-24

| Feature                 | Speaking Answer                                                            | Writing Answer                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sentence length         | Short to medium, varied (natural pauses, \~10–20 words)                    | Longer, complex sentences (\~20–30 words)                                                   |
| Tone                    | Conversational, informal (“Honestly,” “in fact”)                           | Formal, academic, objective                                                                 |
| Common connectors       | “Honestly,” “in fact,” “but,” “so”                                         | “Instead,” “Although,” “With…,” “demonstrates,” “highlighting”                              |
| Vocabulary choice       | Everyday words (“get back,” “hate parties,” “practical”)                   | Advanced/academic terms (“pragmatic,” “enthusiasm,” “retrieve,” “involvement demonstrates”) |
| Emotional expression    | Stronger, with feelings emphasized (“she hates parties,” “honestly”)       | Neutral, downplays emotions, focuses on logic and explanation                               |
| Rhetorical/detail style | Uses contrast and casual commentary, focuses on DVD and dislike of parties | Structured reasoning, highlights cause–effect, contrasts emotions with pragmatic motivation |
