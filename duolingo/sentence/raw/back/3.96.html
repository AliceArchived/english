      1. It wasn't the case that we refused to communicate, sir, but rather that we didn't have any useful information to give you yet.
      2. He thinks he didn't make a mistake.
        1. Main Clause:
          1. It wasn't the case that...
          2. This is a formal, indirect way to negate an assumption.
        2. First Subordinate Clause (Negative):
          1. ...we refused to communicate
          2. Explains what “wasn't the case”
        3. Contrast Connector:
          1. but rather
          2. This introduces the true reason or situation, contrasting the earlier incorrect assumption.
        4. Second Subordinate Clause (Real Reason):
          1. ...that we didn’t have any useful information to give you yet
          2. This is the actual explanation or justification.
        5. Explanation of “but rather that”:
          1. “but rather” is a standard contrast phrase.
          2. What follows is a noun clause starting with “that” → “that we didn’t have any useful information…”
        6. Tip:
          1. “rather that” feels weird because in casual speech we often drop “that” or use simpler contrast:
            1. Formal: but rather that...
            2. Casual: but instead we didn’t... or but actually we didn’t...
