      2. The word "still" is used to indicate that a situation or behavior is continuing up to the present time, often despite expectations for it to have changed. It emphasizes the ongoing nature of an action or state that has been happening and is happening now.
        1. In your sentence, "Mom still loses her patience with me," the use of "still" highlights that despite time passing or possibly efforts to change, the behavior (mom losing her patience) has not changed—it continues up to now.
      3. On the other hand, "will" is used to talk about future actions or states, indicating what someone intends to do or what is likely or expected to happen in the future.
        1. Using "will" would change the meaning. For example, "Mom will lose her patience with me" suggests that this will happen in the future, rather than continuing from the past into the present. It doesn't convey the ongoing or persistent nature of the action that "still" does.
      4. Here are the distinctions:
        1. "Mom still loses her patience with me." — Indicates ongoing action up to the present.
        2. "Mom will lose her patience with me." — Predicts future behavior.
        3. "Still" is more suitable in contexts where the focus is on the continuation of past actions into the present, while "will" is more appropriate when forecasting or planning for what will happen.
