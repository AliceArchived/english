    16. 你的腿可能骨折了。
      1. Your leg probably has broken.
      2. Your leg is probably broken.
        1. better and more idiomatic in English
      3. Verb Tense and Structure: In English, when expressing a state or condition, we often use the simple present tense. Saying "Your leg is probably broken" follows this pattern and is more in line with how English speakers typically convey such information.
      4. Verb Choice: "Has broken" (from the first option) is a present perfect tense, which is generally used to connect a past action with the present. Since the focus is on the current state of the leg (whether it is broken or not), the simple present tense ("is broken") is more appropriate.
      5. Naturalness: "Your leg is probably broken" is a more natural and common way to express this idea in English. It sounds smoother and is easier to understand for native English speakers.
